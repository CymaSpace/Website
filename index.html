Sound-to-Light Visualizer

Translating Sound Frequency into Dynamic Color: Deaf Arts Accessibility Engine

Sound-to-Light Chromatic Translation: Deaf and Hard of Hearing individuals experience music through sight and physical vibration. This visualizer maps incoming sound directly from 0 Hz to 20,000 Hz into 0–255 Hue, transitioning continuously from deep red bass (Hue 0 at 0 Hz) across the visible spectrum to luminous purple (Hue 255 at 20 kHz). Each frequency in live speech, music, or synthesized tones is rendered in its true chromatic color in real time.
♫ Control Colors with the Online Tone Generator
Open Tone Generator (szynalski.com) ↗

What it's for & How to use it: Want to test how specific musical frequencies translate into colors? Turn on Live Microphone below, then open the free tone generator on your smartphone, tablet, or a separate browser tab. As you slide the tone frequency from 20 Hz up to 10,000–20,000 Hz through your speaker, watch this visualizer smoothly shift colors in real time from deep Red bass → Yellow → Green → Cyan → Blue → radiant Purple treble!

⚠
Why your microphone might flip frequencies above 10 kHz: Most consumer built-in laptop and smartphone microphones are physically tuned for speech and have automatic hardware capsules and browser noise filters that steeply roll off frequencies above 8–10 kHz. When a high-pitch tone exceeds 10 kHz, your mic may be unable to register the signal, causing the visualizer to pick up background room noise or sub-harmonics (~300–500 Hz). If this occurs, keep your range set to 10 kHz, or connect an external studio measurement microphone.

Sensory Substitution and Deaf Accessibility

How Direct Frequency-to-Hue (0–255) Translation Enables Deaf Inclusion

Hearing sound is only one way to experience acoustic vibration. By mapping the audible range directly to 0–255 Hue—from deep red bass at 0 Hz all the way to radiant purple at maximum frequency—Deaf and Hard of Hearing individuals can visually track pitch, rhythm, vocal melodies, and musical arrangement with absolute chromatic fidelity.

0–250 Hz • Deep Red

Deep Bass: Crimson Reds

Bass, Kick Drums, Sub-bass, Seismic Rhythms

Mapped to Hue 0 (pure red) up to deep vermilion. These deep frequencies generate physical vibrations felt through bone conduction and vibrohaptic flooring. Rendering them in deep red visually mirrors the visceral physical impact felt by Deaf audiences.

Mid Frequencies • Green & Blue

Melodic Frequencies: Green & Blue

Human Voice, Guitars, Pianos, Melodic Harmonies

Mapped smoothly across yellow, green, cyan, and blue. The core conversational and melodic range of speech and music, allowing viewers to track vocal inflection and harmonic contours through smooth, intuitive color shifts.

Max Treble • Radiant Purple

Air & Treble: Luminous Purples

Cymbals, Brass Overtones, Sibilants, Percussive Attack

Mapped up to Hue 255 (radiant purple) at the acoustic ceiling. Fast percussive transients, cymbals, and airy acoustic details that are often completely inaudible to Deaf individuals are rendered in brilliant violet and purple light.

How CymaSpace Applies This Technology: We embed these frequency-to-light translation algorithms into sound-reactive stage lighting, Pixelblaze-controlled LED matrices, and tactile venue installations. At live concerts, theater productions, and community gatherings at the Woodstock Community Cafe, Deaf, Hard of Hearing, and hearing guests share an identical, synchronized sensory experience.
Physics of Light & Sound

Electromagnetic Spectrum vs. Acoustic Sound Waves

Sound and light belong to completely different physical domains: sound is a mechanical pressure wave propagating through physical matter (air, water, bone), while light is oscillating electromagnetic radiation propagating through space. Explore the interactive canvas below to see the microscopic sliver that human eyes can see, compare it to the acoustic range human ears work for, and see how CymaSpace proportionally maps 0 Hz to Red and Max Sound Hz to Purple.

☝ Drag to pan • Scroll / pinch to zoom • Hover over bands to inspect

● Visible Light: 400 nm to 700 nm

The visible spectrum is a tiny fraction of the electromagnetic spectrum ($\sim 4.3\times 10^{14}$ Hz to $7.5\times 10^{14}$ Hz). Red light is the lowest visible electromagnetic frequency (longest wavelength, ~700 nm), while Purple/Violet light is the highest visible frequency (shortest wavelength, ~400 nm).

● Acoustic Sound: 0 Hz to 10k–20k Hz

Mechanical vibration detectable by human ear hair cells and bone conduction. Deep sub-bass (0–60 Hz) represents the base floor of human acoustic perception, while upper treble (10k–20k Hz) represents the physiological ceiling of human hearing.

● The CymaSpace Sensory Bridge

Because humans cannot directly see acoustic vibrations, CymaSpace proportional mapping aligns the biological thresholds: Lowest Audible Sound (0 Hz) → Lowest Visible Light (Red), and Upper Hearing Ceiling → Highest Visible Light (Purple), giving sound an intuitive visual geometry.

Our Foundation

What is CymaSpace?

We combine scientific study, Deaf culture, community advocacy, and emerging hardware to build a more equitable, vibrant world.

Accessibility & Inclusion

SignKids: Bridging Communication Through Art

SignKids addresses language deprivation by creating an immersive, joyful ASL learning environment for Deaf and Hard of Hearing youth and their families. Enriched with storytelling, puppetry, Deaf mentorship, and hands-on creative play.

Focus: Natural ASL acquisition, cultural pride & family bonding

Sessions: Small cohorts with Deaf mentors and full ADA accessibility
