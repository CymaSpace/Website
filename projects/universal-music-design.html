Accessible Music & Spatial Haptics

Universal Music Design (UMD)

Enhancing the accessibility of dance, music, and performance for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing community. By combining Universal Design principles with spatial haptic vibration technology and wearable gesture instruments, UMD transforms music from an exclusive auditory experience into a tangible, full-body sensation.

Visit UMD site → Gestolumina GitHub Prezi Presentation

Our Vision: Beyond Auditory Sound

Music has historically been defined almost exclusively as an auditory phenomenon, creating systemic barriers for Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) individuals. Universal Music Design (UMD) rejects this limitation. Music is rhythm, frequency, geometry, vibration, and emotional resonance.

Guided by Universal Design principles, UMD creates tools, instruments, and environments where musical expression is felt on the skin, mapped to synchronized chromatic light, and controlled through physical movement. Our mission is to ensure that anyone, regardless of hearing ability, can independently compose, perform, dance to, and experience music in its fullest fidelity.

Core Goals of Universal Music Design

Five foundational pillars guiding our research, hardware development, and community programming.

1. Multi-Sensory Translation

Translating sound waves across sensory modalities by combining tactile spatial vibration, synchronized visual lighting arrays, and acoustic feedback into a cohesive aesthetic experience.

2. Spatial Channel Separation

Preventing complex compositions from degrading into muddied white noise by distributing audio across discrete directional haptic channels (left/right, chest, back, and limbs).

3. Deaf-Led Production

Equipping Deaf music producers and DJs with precision vibrotactile monitoring (SubPac, tactile subwoofers) to independently track, balance, master, and perform live music.

4. Movement-to-Music Tech

Creating intuitive wearable instruments that transform natural bodily gestures and dance into real-time sound synthesis, lighting patterns, and haptic pulses.

5. Accessible Cultural Spaces

Deploying vibrating floors, haptic gear fleets, and visualizers at live venues and festivals, ensuring public performances are welcoming and inclusive for all attendees.

Universal Design Framework

Building accessibility in from the design phase rather than retrofitting afterthoughts, setting an open benchmark for music tech manufacturers and venues worldwide.

Key Innovations & Initiatives

Flagship Wearable Interface

Gestolumina (GeLu)

GitHub Repository → View Prezi →

Gestolumina (GeLu) is a modular musical interface engineered to redefine how human bodies interact with sound. The system features two specialized styles of wearable bracelets: one powered by Pixelblaze and the other by ESP32 microcontrollers, integrated alongside a fingertip ring sensor.

Through onboard IMU and gesture sensors, GeLu detects subtle finger, wrist, and arm movements, immediately converting physical gestures into synthesizer audio, synchronized addressable LED visuals, and haptic feedback. By bridging gesture, light, and vibration, wearers do not just listen to music: their movements become audible, turning the human body into an expressive instrument.

Key Capabilities:
  • Real-time gesture sensing via dual Pixelblaze and ESP32 architectures
  • Direct fingertip and wrist haptic stimulation paired with dynamic color-mapped LED feedback
  • Open-source hardware codebase available for community iteration and educational workshops
Spatial Haptic Fleet

Woojer Vest 3 Fleet & Haptic Event Rental

Universal Music Design operates a specialized rental fleet of 50 high-end Woojer Vest 3 units. Designed for festivals, dance events, concerts, and educational workshops, these vests deliver multi-point spatial haptics across the torso.

By separating stereo audio and frequency bands into discrete physical locations, wearers can perceive sub-bass punch, rhythm lines, and mid-range melodies independently across their chest and back. This spatial clarity provides an authentic connection to musical arrangements that avoids auditory sensory overload.

Deaf-Led Studio Legacy & Future New Studio Buildout in Progress

Confluence Recording Studio & New Studio Buildout

Originally founded in the Portland area as Confluence Recording Co. by Deaf music producer Nate Hergert (DJ Deafwish) and supported by CymaSpace alongside the Oregon Community Foundation Creative Heights Award, Confluence made history as one of the world's first recording studios operated by a Deaf artist. Utilizing tactile subwoofers, SubPac body monitors, and visual feedback arrays, it proved that deafness is no barrier to high-end audio mastering and electronic composition.

When funding ran out, the original Portland Confluence studio closed down, and Universal Music Design currently operates without an open physical studio. However, this vision is actively moving forward: a brand-new studio facility is currently being built out to continue our Deaf-led tactile audio production, spatial haptic testing, and multi-sensory recording initiatives.

Future Research

The Evolution Toward Full-Body Vibrotactile Suits

Building upon the success of bracelet controllers and torso vests, UMD researchers are actively prototyping whole-body vibrotactile garments. The vision encompasses head-to-toe sensory immersion, allowing musical arrangements, orchestral dynamics, and spatial acoustics to wash across the skin with nuance, resolution, and emotional depth.

Published Academic Research

Peer-reviewed papers authored by the Universal Music Design research team advancing the academic and scientific foundations of sensory accessibility.

NIME 2024 Conference Paper DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.13904794

GestoLumina: Gesture interpreted Light, Sound and Haptics. Towards a Framework for Universal Music Design

Authors: Nate Hergert, Dillon Simeone, Doğa Çavdır, Duncan MacConnell, Shawn Trail, Myles de Bastion

Published in the Proceedings of the International Conference on New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIME 2024). This paper presents GestoLumina (GeLu), detailing its modular architecture, tactile ring sensor, audio-reactive LED wristbands, and interaction design frameworks tailored specifically to enhance musical education and performance for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing community.

ACM ASSETS Conference Paper SIGACCESS Accessibility

Sonic Agency: A Group Autoethnography of Technology-mediated Performance Practice by Deaf and Hard of Hearing Musicians

Authors: Dillon Simeone, Shawn Trail, Doğa Çavdır, and collaborators

Published in the ACM SIGACCESS Conference on Computers and Accessibility (ASSETS). This research presents a group autoethnography exploring how Deaf and Hard of Hearing musicians appropriate, perform with, and co-design technology-mediated sonic practices. It documents how spatial haptics, tactile subwoofers, and visualizers enable artists to reclaim creative agency and define new modalities of musical artistry beyond auditory listening.

The Team

Meet the researchers, engineers, writers, and producers driving Universal Music Design.

PI Lead

Shawn Trail, PhD

Principal Investigator & Research Lead

Percussionist, audio engineer, and computer scientist. Shawn leads the overarching research trajectory, experimental testing, and academic collaborations for Universal Music Design. His work bridges acoustic signal processing, machine learning, physical computing, and haptic interface development, creating empirical methodologies for multi-sensory music accessibility.

Design Lead

Dillon Simeone

Lead Design Engineer

A hardware and software engineer from the Deaf community, Dillon specializes in audio-reactive haptic systems, embedded microcontrollers (ESP32, Pixelblaze), and wearable accessibility tech. He is the lead designer of the Gestolumina (GeLu) gesture-sensing haptic bracelets and co-author on technology-mediated performance practice for Deaf musicians.

Operations

Tracy Held

Project Coordinator

An award-winning writer for stage and screen with an MFA in Dramatic Writing from Carnegie Mellon University, co-founder of Erosion (a climate-conscious film production company), and veteran nonprofit marketing and fundraising consultant. Tracy orchestrates grant development, project administration, community partnerships, and strategic storytelling for Universal Music Design.

Music Maker

Nate Hergert

Music Maker & Deaf DJ (DJ Deafwish)

Deaf electronic music producer, DJ, and founder of Confluence Recording Studio. A fellow of the Oregon Community Foundation Creative Heights Award, Nate pioneers tactile mixing and performance methodologies using SubPac vibrotactile feedback, crafting bass-driven electronic music that resonates deeply across sensory boundaries.
